### Section 2

The principal enactment is amended by the insertion after section 2, of “Application ofFiscal Responsibility Rules

Fiscal responsibility rules

### Section 2A

The following numerical fiscal responsibility rules shall apply in the management of public finances:

fhj

an annual positive primary balance on commitment basis of at least one point five per cent of the Gross Domestic Product shall be maintained; and

(b)

the public debt to Gross Domestic Product ratio of forty-five percent or lower shall be achieved by the year 2034.

Suspension of fiscal responsibility rules

### Section 2B

(1) The Minister may, with the prior approval of Parliament, suspend the fiscal responsibility rules due to

(a)

a force majeure\

(b)

an unforeseen economic circumstance; or

(c)

a force majeure and an unforeseen economic circumstance.

(2) The Minister shall suspend the fiscal responsibility rules pursuant to subsection (1) only where the Minister is of the opinion that the implementation of the fiscal responsibility rules shall be harmful to the fiscal, macroeconomic or financial stability of the country.

(3) Where the Minister suspends the fiscal responsibility rules, the Minister shall, within one month after the occurrence of the force majeure or unforeseen economic circumstance, submit to Parliament for approval, a memorandum in support of the justification for the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules.

(4)

The memorandum shall include

(a)

the facts and circumstances that gave rise to the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules in a given financial year;

(b)

the fiscal impact of the shock on lives and livelihoods;

(c)

plans for the restoration of the public finances and fiscal responsibility rules of the country within one year after theforce majeure or unforeseen economic circumstance has elapsed; and

(d)

a quarterly break down of the revised primary balance target for the year as part of the budget presented to Parliament.

(5) The Council established under section 11A shall provide an assessment on whether any of the circumstances specified in subsection (1) that justify the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules was present in order to inform the decision of Parliament in the grant of an approval for the suspension of the fiscal responsibility; rules.

(6) Where Parliament is on recess, Cabinet may approve the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules but the approval shall be ratified by Parliament during the next sitting of Parliament after the recess.

(7) Where Parliament approves or ratifies the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules, the Minister shall, within fourteen days after the grant of the approval or the ratification of the approval, publish the memorandum for justification of the suspension of the fiscal responsibility rules on the official website of the Ministry.

(8) Where the Minister is of the opinion that a restoration of the fiscal responsibility rules in a given financial year may take more than one year after the force majeure or unforeseen economic circumstance has elapsed, the Minister shall request from Parliament an additional time required for the restoration.

(9) A request under subsection (8) shall be accompanied with justification for the request.

(10)

For the purpose of this section,

(a)

'force majeure” includes a natural disaster, a public health emergency including a pandemic, a drought or a war where a state of emergency has been declared by the President under article 31 of the Constitution; and

(b)

“unforeseen economic circumstance” includes

(i)

an unanticipated severe economic shock including a commodity price shock;

(ii)

a domestic or global financial crisis or shock that severely affects the bank and non-bank financial system of the country; and

(iii)

a period where the country records an actual negative year-on-year real Gross Domestic Product growth in two successive quarters.

Breach of fiscal responsibility rules

### Section 2C

(1) Where a fiscal responsibility rule is breached, the Minister shall

(a)

publish on the official website of die Ministry, the reason for the breach; and

(b)

within four months after the end of the financial year in which the rule was breached, submit a detailed corrective action plan to Parliament and Cabinet.

(2) Where a fiscal responsibility rule is breached, the next budget to be approved by Parliament shall contain the requisite corrective fiscal measures.

(3) Where the primary balance rule is breached, a new discretionary project including an infrastructure project that is not essential for a basic service shall not be admitted into the budget until the fiscal situation is corrected.

(4) Where the fiscal responsibility rule on public debt threshold is breached, the Minister shall set and implement limits on new borrowing until the debt threshold slippage is corrected.

(5) Where the Minister breaches one or more of the fiscal responsibility rules by more than one percentage point in two successive years, Parliament may, in accordance with article 82 of the Constitution, pass a vote of censure on the Minister.

(6) The head of a covered entity or a Minister of State whose action or inaction contributes to a fiscal slippage shall be sanctioned in accordance with sections 96 to 98 of the Public Financial Management Act, 2016 (Act 921).

Review of fiscal responsibility rules

### Section 2D

The Minister shall, after the end of every five years, submit to Parliament a review of the fiscal responsibility rules specified in section 2A.”. Sections 11A to 11O inserted

Section 102 of Act 921 amended

### Section 11

The principal enactment is amended in section 102 by the insertion of the following definitions in the appropriate alphabetical order: ““annual positive primary balance” means the positive difference between revenues and non-interest expenditures for a fiscal year;

“Council” means the Fiscal Council established under section 11 A;

“distorted incentives” means incentives that are counter to the fiscal objectives in the national budget; “economic shock” means a sudden and unexpected event that significantly impacts an economy positively or negatively;

“expenditure overages” mean excess expenditure over programmed expenditure targets;

“fiscal responsibility rules” means a long-standing constraint on fiscal policy through numerical limits on budgetary aggregates; “fiscal slippage” means the failure to achieve a fiscal target in the national budget; “net change in stock of payables” means accumulation of new payables minus arrears clearance over a period; “non-interest expenditure” means expenditure excluding interest payments;

“price shocks” means a sudden appreciable increase or decrease in price; “primary balance on commitment basis” means the difference between revenues and non-interest expenditures on cash basis plus net change in stock of payables; “public debt” means a guaranteed debt of the central Government; and “severe economic shock” means an economic shock that causes a year-on-year real Gross Domestic Product growth rate to contract in two successive quarters.”.
